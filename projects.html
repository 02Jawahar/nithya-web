MYP Personal Project

EcoSAT — a satellite designed to clean up after itself.

A CubeSat that de-orbits itself automatically at the end of its mission, so it burns up harmlessly in the atmosphere instead of adding to the debris field.

EcoSAT exhibition pictures

Why I took it on

I came across the Kessler syndrome — the theory that collisions between pieces of space debris can set off a chain reaction that eventually surrounds Earth in enough debris to make future space exploration unsafe. Once you’ve read that, a satellite that just stops working and stays in orbit stops looking neutral. It looks like a contribution to the problem.

How the design works

EcoSAT uses a cold-gas propulsion system: pressurised gas is expelled through a nozzle and generates enough thrust to lower the satellite’s orbit until it burns up harmlessly in the atmosphere, instead of adding to the debris field.

I researched the problem and worked with the local maker space to understand the various components — and the materials that could be used to prototype the cube satellite.

The problem

Kessler syndrome — cascading collisions between orbital debris.

The mechanism

Cold-gas propulsion: pressurised gas through a nozzle, thrust enough to lower the orbit.

The build

Component and materials research with a local maker space, toward a CubeSat prototype.
