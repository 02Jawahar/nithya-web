R&D Intern · Banyan Nation · 2025 & 2026

Two summers inside a patented plastics recycling pioneer.

I researched two connected problems: what contamination does to recycled polyethylene, and what gets left behind in the water after the plastic is washed.

Read the full report Photos from the lab
Research strand 01 Summer 2025 & 2026
FTIR · DSC

Contamination in recycled polyethylene

Composition & thermal behaviour of recycled pellets

Recycled low-density and high-density polyethylene (LDPE and HDPE) frequently comes contaminated with polypropylene. I used FTIR — Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy — and DSC — differential scanning calorimetry — to understand how different contamination levels change the composition and thermal behaviour of recycled plastic pellets: their rigidity, their flexibility, and their strength under heat.

  • FTIR spectra to identify polymer composition and contaminant signatures across sample batches.
  • DSC to trace how melting and crystallisation behaviour shifts as polypropylene content rises.
  • Mapped contamination level against the mechanical properties that matter downstream.
Research strand 02 Wastewater treatment
Coagulants & flocculants

Inefficiency in the plastic-washing process

Suspended particles & microplastics in wastewater

The second problem was the washing process itself — particularly the suspended particles and microplastics left behind in the wastewater. I worked with coagulants and flocculants, including polyacrylamide, polyaluminium chloride, and sulfates, to try to improve the treatment process.

  • Trialled polyacrylamide, polyaluminium chloride, and sulfate-based treatments.
  • Targeted the suspended-solid and microplastic load that survives conventional washing.
Outcome End of summer 2026

From FTIR scan to a view on where the field should go

Synthesis & forward-looking review

By the end of my second summer, I synthesized the emerging trends in this space and the techniques that seemed most promising going forward. The part of the experience I’m proudest of is moving from running an FTIR scan to arguing for where the field should go next.
